1 · Fit: your name is the model

Thousands of points start as a random Gaussian cloud and are trained into your name. The loss counter in the corner is real: it's the actual mean squared distance of the points from their targets, so it rises when you move your mouse through the letters and falls as they re-converge. The message: "this person fits models", told without a single word of copy.
Watch out for: a few particle-text effects exist on the web; the live loss counter and the "training" framing are what make this one yours.

2 · Descent: an optimizer running behind your name

Hundreds of particles run stochastic gradient descent with momentum on a real loss surface (faint contour bands). The surface slowly drifts, so they never stop searching. Click anywhere to drop a batch of new particles and watch them roll into the minima. It reads as "optimization, statistics, markets" and ties straight to your MIT Optimization course.
Watch out for: the most "ambient" of the four. Beautiful, but the name itself is static text.

3 · Life: your name inside Conway's Game of Life

Your name assembles out of cells, then sits inside a live cellular automaton. The letters count as living cells, so patterns grow, collide and die around them. Move your mouse to seed new life. It reads as "computer scientist who likes systems and emergence" and connects to the intro animation.
Watch out for: if the intro also uses Game of Life, this one should be the intro's ending (they'd be one continuous animation, not two).

4 · Nixie: your name on a vintage lab instrument

Your name in glowing nixie tubes, the 1960s lab display that Steins;Gate's divergence meter uses. On load each tube rolls through characters like the meter does, then settles. Hover a tube to re-roll it. Non-fans see a beautiful retro scientific instrument; fans recognize it instantly. This is the most "this is exactly who Franco is" option: lab nerd, anime, and craft.
Watch out for: it changes the theme's accent from green to warm orange. It says less about "quantitative" by itself, so the work carousel underneath has to carry that.

Work carousel

Replaces the single featured project. Cards orbit slowly on a 3D ring, with cards at the back dimmed and the front card bright. Hovering pauses it, and clicking a card opens its case study. It's slow on purpose: it should feel like something drifting in a lab, not a slideshow.
On mobile it becomes a simple swipe row.

Cursor ideas

All four: desktop only (touch screens keep the normal behaviour), the normal text cursor stays over text you can select, and every one is a small canvas overlay, so it's cheap. My pick is B for the quant signal and humour, or A if you want it quieter. More ideas not built here: a cursor that "samples" whatever it hovers (shows the colour hex / element name like a probe); a tiny "Upa" (Steins;Gate) that follows on the 404 page only; a magnifying glass on /life photos (Professor Layton).
