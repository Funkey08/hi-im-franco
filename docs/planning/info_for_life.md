Brief explanations for books:
- Kafka on the Shore by Murakami - Murakami is probably my favourite author of all time. Reading his books make me realize that you don't need to understand everything, all the time - simply experiencing the moment is enough. I talked about him for 20 seconds in a YouTube video once [[hyperlink](https://youtu.be/MPC2psjfJ0E?si=hoEfl-OOlY6heOiS&t=285)], but I've written entire essays (for fun) about these books, and I could talk about them for hours.
- Three Body Problem - Cixin Liu made me think existentially about science, discovery, and the future of humanity. This was an amazing series to nerd out over. The way he intertwines science, politics, psychology, game theory, and virtually every other field chronicled over hundreds and thousands of years made me appreciate (and fear) humanity's progress as a collective team effort.
- Lord of the Rings - What else can I say? I've never read any other author whose worlds are as rich as the ones Tolkien builds. It's almost overwhelming, and yet there's so much beauty and depth behind every tree, every meadow, every river, every creature. If you want to get lost in an adventure, this is it.
- Dune - I don't know why this book ed with me, but it did. The universe in Dune is a massive one, with hundreds of thousands of years of history and enriched with unbelievably complex factions, cultures, and character dynamics. And yet Dune focuses on a specific story, about a specific character, on a specific planet. It's a bit surreal to think about how the actions of the characters on this specific planet have the power to affect an entire galaxy, and I think Paul Atreides, the center of it all, realizes this. I think we all feel like Paul Atreides sometimes. 
- Exhalation - Every single one of Ted Chiang's stories explores the effects that knowledge, science, and technology have on the human psyche. I feel that Chiang has a way of tearing into raw cores of metaphysical concepts - whether it be mortality, spirituality, truth, or the flow of time - through such concise and simple stories.
